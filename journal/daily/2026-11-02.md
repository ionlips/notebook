## How the article handles references

Per project, it creates two sections in every project:
- **Later** (Depois) holds future steps that aren't actionable yet.
- **Notes** (Notas) holds reference material for that project.

Any task whose title starts with `*` becomes a plain text item with no checkbox. So the Notes section can hold things like the project's purpose, desired outcome, brainstorm, and links, without cluttering your task lists.

Project-specific notes live inside the project. Cross-cutting references (Books, Recipes, Contacts) stay in a global **Reference** project.

The author builds the sections from a reusable template based on Allen's Natural Planning Model. The Notes section starts with items like:
- `* Purpose & Principles`
- `* Outcome / Vision`
- `* Brainstorm`
- `* Organising`

Then you save the project as a template and import it whenever you start a new project.

## How I'd adapt it for you

**Project template**
```
Sections:
  Next      (the next action, with @next)
  Later     (future steps, no labels)
  Notes     (reference, items prefixed with "* ")
```
Items for Notes:
- `* Outcome` (what "done" looks like)
- `* Purpose`
- `* Brainstorm`
- `* Links & files` (attach files or add comments here)

Todoist also lets you attach files and links as project comments. Use those for documents, and use `*` items for short written notes.

**Keep the global Reference project.** Use it for things that don't belong to any one project, like gift ideas or contact information. Also keep your weekly review checklist there.

**Keep the `@next` label.** The article doesn't use one. It treats everything outside Later and Notes as actionable. That's less work (no labels to maintain), but it means every filter needs exclusions. `@next` makes filters shorter and more precise, so I'd keep it. The Later section still helps by making future steps visible without tagging them.

## Filter changes

The filters built on `@next` need no change, since notes never carry that label. Filters that look at dates or priorities could pick up notes (e.g. a Notes item with a due date), so add `& !/Notes` to those:

| Filter            | Updated query                                                                                 |
| ----------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Today focus       | `(today \| overdue \| @next) & !@waiting_for & !##Someday & !/Notes`                          |
| Q1 Do             | `p1 & !##Someday & !/Notes`                                                                   |
| Q2 Schedule       | `p2 & no date & !##Someday & !/Notes`                                                         |
| Q3 Delegate       | `p3 & !##Someday & !/Notes`                                                                   |
| Eisenhower matrix | `p1 & !##Someday & !/Notes, p2 & !##Someday & !/Notes, p3 & !##Someday & !/Notes, p4 & @next` |
| Overdue           | `overdue & !/Notes`                                                                           |
| Coming week       | `next 7 days & !@waiting_for & !/Notes`                                                       |
| Q2 not scheduled  | `p2 & no date & !/Notes`                                                                      |

New filters for the weekly review:

| Filter          | Query    | What it's for                                                                                       |
| --------------- | -------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Later items** | `/Later` | All parked future steps across projects. Promote one to Next when a project's current step is done. |
| **Notes**       | `/Notes` | All reference items. Useful to skim, but not for daily use.                                         |

I took the `/Notes` section syntax from the article and haven't tested it, so verify it in Todoist. Names matter, so the section must be spelled exactly `Notes` and `Later`.

## Other ideas from the article, if you want them
- **Size labels** (`@S`, `@M`, `@L`) for effort, to filter by how much time and energy you have.
- **A Calendar project** for hard-landscape items, with the weekly review as a recurring task in it.
- **Weekly review as a template**: the article creates a fresh "Weekly Review" project from a template each week, works through it, then archives it. Your completed reviews stay in the archive, and every run starts from a clean checklist.
